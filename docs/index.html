Train a Mixture-of-Experts LM and Watch It: Balance, Diagnostics, Upcycling and Serving

How it was built

Part 1: A Corpus with Planted Domains

A seeded generator of token sequences from eight domains, each with its own next-token rules and a tag token that announces it, plus the exact entropy floor that no model can beat. The domains are the structure experts can specialise on, and the floor turns every loss in the project into a gap you can read.

Part 2: A Dense Reference

The feed-forward layer every expert is a smaller copy of, and a two-layer pre-norm decoder that takes any feed-forward module, so the dense model and every mixture-of-experts variant share the same attention, embeddings and head.

Part 3: The Router

How router logits become expert choices and mixing weights (gating function, renormalization, scaling factor, a selection-only bias and a serving-time denominator), the two router losses (z-loss and the load-balancing loss) and the capacity rule that drops assignments when an expert overflows.

Part 4: The Mixture-of-Experts Layer

Assemble the router, the experts, the router losses and the capacity rule into one drop-in feed-forward module that records what it did on every forward pass, and count the parameters a token actually uses against the parameters the model stores.

Part 5: What to Watch

The signals the MoE training handbook says to watch, one function each: MaxVio for imbalance, the router's entropy, top weight and routed output size for its sharpness, the largest and smallest expert output norm over the median for rogue and dead experts, and domain purity for specialisation, gathered per layer into one dashboard.

Part 6: Balancing and Training

The two bias-based balancers that move load without any gradient (the fixed-step update and quantile balancing), an evaluation loop, and one training loop that runs every variant in the study: no balancing, the aux loss, the loss-free bias or quantile balancing, with optional z-loss, a monitor and a planted bug.

Part 7: Upcycling and a Quiet Failure

Start a mixture of experts from a trained dense model so it computes exactly the dense function at step 0, then build a run monitor with the handbook's alarms and plant a bug that grows one expert while the loss keeps falling, to see which signal catches it.

Part 8: Serving and the Full Study

Serve a trained top-2 model with one expert per token under three choices of denominator, then run the whole study in one function: dense against matched MoE, five router and balancing settings with their dashboards, upcycling, the capacity factor, serving at a smaller k and the planted failure.
